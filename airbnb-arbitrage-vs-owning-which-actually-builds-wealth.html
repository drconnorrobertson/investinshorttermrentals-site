Airbnb Arbitrage vs. Owning: Which Actually Builds Wealth

Published 2026-09-25 · Invest in Short Term Rentals Editorial

Arbitrage is a cash flow strategy with no equity upside

Airbnb arbitrage -- subleasing a property and operating it as a short-term rental with the landlord's permission -- can generate monthly cash flow with a lower upfront capital requirement than buying outright, but it builds no equity and no depreciation benefit, and it ends the moment the lease does.

It also carries lease-dependent risk: a landlord can decline to renew, or local regulation can restrict subleased short-term rentals specifically, in ways that don't affect an owned property the same way.

Ownership builds equity, depreciation benefit and long-term control

Owning a short-term rental requires more capital upfront but builds equity through appreciation and principal paydown, unlocks depreciation and cost segregation benefits arbitrage can't access, and isn't subject to a landlord's decision to end the arrangement.

For an investor with access to capital and a longer time horizon, ownership through a done-for-you acquisition process typically builds more durable wealth than arbitrage -- though arbitrage can be a reasonable way to learn short-term rental operations with less capital at risk first.
