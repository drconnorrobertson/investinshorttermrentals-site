Reconcile calendar nights and accommodation revenue before testing demand

This is a separate, original fictional record exercise, not the property’s base illustration, a market estimate or an observed booking result. The phrase “accessible-layout bungalow” in this worksheet’s title does not establish any actual property’s accessibility, legal compliance, price premium or demand. Verify the intended use, physical features and guest requirements from actual records; no such observations are supplied here.

1. Keep available nights separate from calendar nights

Assume a 365-day calendar, 25 owner-blocked nights and 10 nights out of service. Those blocks do not overlap, leaving 330 nights offered for sale. Assume 165 paid, occupied nights; canceled reservations without a stay are excluded. On the offered-night denominator, occupancy is 165 ÷ 330 = 50%. On a full-calendar denominator it is 165 ÷ 365 ≈ 45.21%. These describe the same stays using different denominators, not a change in demand.

This particular worksheet’s displayed model uses 365 available nights and does not offer a blocked-night input. To reproduce the exercise’s 165 paid nights in that model, enter 165/365 as a decimal, approximately 0.4520547945205479, rather than 0.50. Retain 330 and the 50% offered-night measure in your separate calendar review. Do not call the converted input an occupancy forecast or silently replace one denominator with the other.

2. Bridge room charges to a realized accommodation rate

For this exercise, the lodging ledger contains $49,500 of accommodation charges before $3,300 of price discounts and $1,650 of accommodation refunds. The refunds concern the same 165 occupied nights and do not remove nights from that count. Taxes, guest cleaning charges, deposits and platform fees are outside this revenue bridge. The assumed net accommodation amount is $44,550, or $270 per paid night. A payout total after platform deductions is a different measure.

3. More paid nights need not improve contribution

Now consider an alternative assumption of 190 paid nights at a $240 realized accommodation rate, giving $45,600. On the same 330 offered nights this is about 57.58% occupancy; in this 365-night worksheet, use 190/365, approximately 0.5205479452054794. This is a hypothetical alternative, not evidence that reducing a rate causes those bookings.

The accommodation-revenue increase is only $45,600 − $44,550 = $1,050. Suppose those extra nights require ten additional turnovers at $85 of owner-paid net cleaning cost each, plus $500 of additional energy. The partial incremental contribution is $1,050 − $850 − $500 = −$300, before management, booking fees, maintenance and other costs. This partial calculation is not annual owner cash flow; the full worksheet also includes financing, fixed bills and replacement reserves. Keep guest cleaning receipts separate so they are not counted as both room revenue and an offset to cleaning expense.

Calculator scope: changing only nightly rate and occupancy reproduces the alternative’s accommodation revenue, not its added turnover and energy costs. This worksheet has no separate net-cleaning input on this topic. Reconcile the $850 cleaning amount outside it, and update the relevant utility assumption separately if you use the $500 energy assumption. Do not present the resulting rate/occupancy-only cash-flow output as including all of this exercise’s extra costs.

4. Replace every assumed record before using the result

  • Reconcile the same dates across the booking calendar, occupied-night count and accommodation ledger.
  • Identify overlapping owner blocks, maintenance closures, cancellations and complimentary stays rather than treating every empty date alike.
  • Document discounts and refunds once, and reconcile platform deductions to payouts separately.
  • Quote the additional service and operating costs of each alternative; no price, booking or accessibility premium is established by this exercise.
