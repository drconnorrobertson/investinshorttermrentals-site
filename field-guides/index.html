Invest in Short Term Rentals field guides

Practical evidence checks, worked examples and editable worksheets for portfolio research and capital planning.

6 guides

Build a portfolio cash commitment calendar

Annual investment totals can hide months when several obligations occur together. A commitment calendar places known payments, reserve transfers and planned project draws on dates. Keep expected operating receipts distinct from cash already available, and label uncertain dates so a model does not imply funding certainty.

Map confidence in underwriting sources

Not every model input deserves the same confidence. A source map records whether a number comes from reconciled history, a current quote, a comparable or an unsupported estimate. The aim is to make uncertainty visible and prioritize research, not to turn a subjective score into a promised investment outcome.

Bridge planned and actual property capital

A capital bridge explains how the original approved budget became the actual amount funded. Separate scope additions, price changes, timing changes and reserves still held. Keep cash paid distinct from unpaid commitments and avoid interpreting a higher capital contribution as an accounting classification.

Schedule reviews of investment assumptions

Inputs become stale at different rates. A signed financing term, an operating quote and an annual revenue estimate should each have a review trigger appropriate to the decision they support. Use a refresh calendar to identify when the model must be updated before the next approval.

Review the evidence behind portfolio concentration

Two properties may share exposure through demand seasonality, management capacity or funding commitments even when their addresses differ. A concentration review identifies those connections using documented operating facts. It organizes questions for the owner rather than assigning an unsupported diversification score.

Keep a decision-focused investment question log

A review meeting becomes more useful when unresolved questions have owners and evidence requirements. Write questions that can be answered with a document, calculation or explicit decision. Preserve answers and source references so the same issue is not repeatedly reopened without new information.
