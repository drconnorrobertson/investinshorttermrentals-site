Portfolio research and capital planning · Field guide · October 9, 2026

Review the evidence behind portfolio concentration

Two properties may share exposure through demand seasonality, management capacity or funding commitments even when their addresses differ. A concentration review identifies those connections using documented operating facts. It organizes questions for the owner rather than assigning an unsupported diversification score.

Work through the decision

  1. List shared operating dependencies.
  2. Compare timing of seasonal cash needs.
  3. Identify reliance on the same providers.
  4. Record which proposed changes require further evidence.

For each action, name the person responsible for preparing the response and the person who approves it. Record what evidence would close the item. If new information changes cost, timing or scope, update the affected plan and identify who needs the revised version.

Common failure to avoid

Do not assume different postal addresses establish independent risks. Describe the actual shared dependency and the effect you want to investigate.

Worked example

An illustrative portfolio uses one manager and the same peak booking season for two homes. The review treats those as shared dependencies while separately examining each property’s budget and vendor coverage.

This is an illustrative workflow, not a client case study or a forecast. Replace the example’s facts with the records for your own project.

Close the handoff

A finished worksheet should identify the result, its evidence and any remaining owner. If the result is uncertain, record the next action instead of marking the whole task complete. Keep the worksheet with the relevant source file and tell the receiving person which version applies.
