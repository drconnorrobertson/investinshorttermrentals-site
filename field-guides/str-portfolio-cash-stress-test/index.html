STR portfolio stress test before buying another rental

Owning three rentals does not automatically create three independent sources of cash. Properties can share a season, booking channel, customer base, local restriction or service provider. Before making another offer, test whether the combined portfolio can fund obligations when more than one property disappoints at the same time.

This worksheet isolates a common revenue decline and a simultaneous cash bill across three properties. It does not estimate the probability of a downturn, recommend a reserve amount or predict market revenue. Replace every input with your own reconciled records and a sensitivity you intentionally want to test.

Define the cash pool before adding properties

Start with cash actually available to support these rentals after the proposed purchase and launch costs. Exclude household emergency funds, operating-company working capital, restricted lender reserves and money already committed to another project. If ownership or lender restrictions prevent transfers between properties, do not treat their accounts as one freely available pool; run separate tests and obtain the relevant approvals.

Use accommodation receipts rather than gross booking totals that include pass-through taxes. Reconcile cleaning collections and cleaning costs consistently so the same expense is not deducted twice. A booked calendar is not necessarily collected cash. The calendar-status analysis explains why owner blocks, confirmed stays and unresolved calendar gaps must remain separate.

Classify expenses by what changes in this scenario

Enter revenue-linked cash costs as a percentage only when they plausibly change with the modeled receipts. Enter debt payments and the fixed bills that remain due in the fixed-cost column. Some expenses are partly variable and partly fixed; split them using the actual agreement instead of assuming all management, utilities or cleaning expenses move together. Include mortgage principal in cash outflow even though principal is not an operating deduction.

The separate repair input is a one-time bill paid at the start of this simplified horizon. Do not add a replacement reserve contribution and the identical funded repair as two new bills. If a repair occurs later, or receipts vary by month, use the dated commitment calendar rather than treating this constant-month tool as an exact forecast.

Worked example: positive base cash can become a reserve draw

All figures below are invented and are not client results, property listings or typical costs. Three properties collect $8,000, $6,500 and $5,000 per month. Each has 25% revenue-linked cash costs. Their fixed bills, including debt service, are $5,500, $4,500 and $3,500 per month.

Illustrative propertyBase monthly cashCash after 20% lower receipts
A$8,000 × 75% − $5,500 = $500$6,400 × 75% − $5,500 = −$700
B$6,500 × 75% − $4,500 = $375$5,200 × 75% − $4,500 = −$600
C$5,000 × 75% − $3,500 = $250$4,000 × 75% − $3,500 = −$500
Total$1,125−$1,800

Over three months, the stressed operation uses $5,400 of cash. Add a distinct $15,000 repair bill at the start: total reserve reduction is $20,400. A $60,000 starting reserve ends at $39,600. Against an owner-chosen $45,000 floor, the shortfall is $5,400. The floor is an example constraint, not a recommendation.

The deterioration from the base operating case is $8,775 over three months: ($1,125 − (−$1,800)) × 3. That is different from the $5,400 actual operating cash draw. Do not add forgone positive cash as another invoice. This distinction matters when sizing the funding actually needed before bills come due.

Interpret the result before choosing a purchase

  • Positive stressed cash: the included receipts exceed included bills under this sensitivity. That does not establish completeness of costs, legal use or investment suitability.
  • Negative stressed cash: identify who can fund the dated gap and whether that cash is genuinely accessible. Do not assume a favorable later month arrives before the next payment.
  • Below the chosen floor: compare a lower-capital purchase, a smaller verified setup scope, delayed acquisition or a different supported operating plan. Do not simply lower the floor to make the model pass.
  • Concentrated exposure: identify the source of the shared shock. Buying in another ZIP code may not diversify the same weather season, employer demand or operating dependency.

Documents to bring to an acquisition discussion

Bring property-level monthly receipts, payout timing, fixed bills, management fee definitions, condition-driven replacement estimates, debt terms and the proposed purchase-to-launch cash plan. Keep private financial records in agreed secure channels. Record the date and source of each input, the stress you selected and the event that would cause you to update it.

For financing, the CFPB Loan Estimate explainer helps readers understand applicable mortgage disclosures. Loan products and disclosure requirements differ, so use the actual lender documents for the investment loan. A lender’s reserve calculation is not the same as this portfolio operating stress.

Read the portfolio concentration review and portfolio expansion guide. If you are actively comparing a next acquisition, discuss your buy box and verified funding limits with BNB Accelerator. Ask which sourcing, underwriting and launch tasks are included in the written scope before engaging.
