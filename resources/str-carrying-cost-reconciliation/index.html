STR Carrying Costs: Reconcile the Loan Quote and Budget

A lender's payment quote and an investment model answer different questions. Before comparing properties, build an annual carrying-cost file that connects each modeled expense to a dated document. This guide shows how to keep financing, escrow, operating bills and capital reserves visible without counting the same cash outflow twice.

Start with the loan terms that apply to this property

Keep the proposed loan amount, interest rate, term, rate-lock status and repayment structure together. Record which property use and borrower facts the lender assumed. If the lender supplies several versions, identify the version used in your model and retain earlier versions so changes can be explained.

The CFPB Loan Estimate explainer distinguishes principal and interest from the total payment, identifies costs paid outside escrow, and explains estimated cash to close. The explainer describes that consumer mortgage disclosure; do not assume a business-purpose or investor loan provides the same form. Ask your lender which disclosure or term sheet applies and request equivalent cost detail.

Map each expense to where the money leaves

Use separate columns for the annual expense, monthly allowance, payment timing and whether the lender collects it through escrow. Property taxes and insurance should each appear once in the annual expense total, even if they are funded through monthly mortgage payments. If you model a total mortgage payment that already includes those amounts, reconcile the overlap before adding standalone tax and insurance lines.

Association dues, utilities, management, routine maintenance and replacement reserves need their own evidence and timing. A lender's escrow collection is not a complete operating budget. Retain quotes or actual bills and document the period covered, proposed rental use and any known renewal date.

Test a small reconciliation before using a large spreadsheet

Illustrative amounts: annual principal and interest of $24,000, property taxes of $6,000, insurance of $3,600 and association dues of $2,400 produce $36,000 of those carrying costs. If the stated mortgage collection is $2,800 monthly and already includes the $9,600 tax-and-insurance escrow contribution, adding another $9,600 would count that component twice. Confirm the actual quote's contents before using this example.

This illustration excludes management, utilities, repairs, replacements and other expenses. It demonstrates a reconciliation, not a property forecast or a complete return calculation. For the capital denominator, continue with return on total cash invested.

Verify hazard and coverage assumptions separately

Record the address used for the hazard review and ask the insurance professional what the proposed rental activity changes. The USGS flood-map guidance links to FEMA’s official insurance-purpose mapping resources. Keep the map reference, insurer response and quoted deductible as separate evidence; one does not replace the others. Ask which premium, coverage or deductible assumptions should be stressed in a downside case.

Distinguish cash to close from the cash needed to operate

List earnest money already paid, remaining settlement funding, furnishing commitments, launch invoices and retained reserves separately. The lender's closing estimate does not represent every post-closing cost of the rental business. Check the source and due date of each payment so money reserved for launch is not simultaneously shown as emergency liquidity.

Download the carrying-cost reconciliation worksheet. Label unsupported entries as assumptions and assign an owner to obtain evidence. Use remote-owner reporting controls to compare the approved budget with actual bills after launch.
